Two-minute check before you buy

  1. Find the brand and model number.

  2. Search it here, then in the full database at CPSC.gov for older products.

  3. No model number from the seller? Treat that as your answer.

Why it matters for deals

Clearance lots, marketplaces and secondhand listings are where recalled stock is most likely to turn up. A low price on an unfamiliar brand deserves a search first.

Questions people ask

What should I do if I own a recalled product?

Stop using it and open the official notice. Each one explains the remedy, which is usually a refund, a free repair or a replacement, and how to claim it.

Is it legal to sell a recalled product?

No. Selling or reselling a recalled consumer product is against U.S. federal law.

Which recalls are shown here?

Recent recalls of products for the home: furniture, mattresses, appliances, heating and cooling, tools, outdoor equipment, lighting, decor and nursery items. Toys, clothing, vehicles and sports equipment are left out.

How current is this list?

The site checks the Consumer Product Safety Commission’s public recall database four times a day.
