How we work

Data sources, the method behind every signal and the rules our content follows.

This page explains where the numbers on Home.Deals come from, how the signals are calculated and what rules the editorial content follows.

Data sources

What you seeSourceRefreshed
Price PulseU.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers, item indexes, not seasonally adjustedMonthly, as published
Mortgage ratesFreddie Mac Primary Mortgage Market Survey weekly averages, retrieved from FRED, Federal Reserve Bank of St. LouisWeekly
Housing market meterU.S. Census Bureau and HUD new residential sales, and the FHFA house price index, retrieved from FREDMonthly and quarterly
Electricity and gas pricesU.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics average price dataMonthly
Recall RadarU.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission recall databaseSeveral times a day
Consumer alertsU.S. Federal Trade CommissionSeveral times a day
VideosPublic YouTube channel feeds, played in the official YouTube playerSeveral times a day

An automated job collects these figures four times a day and rebuilds the pages only when something has changed. If a source is unavailable, the site keeps showing the last good figures with their date.

How a Price Pulse signal is set

  1. Season. Each category has a set of months when sales are historically strongest. If the current month is one of them, the signal is “Buy window”.
  2. Proximity. If a stronger month is no more than two months away, the signal is “Wait”.
  3. Otherwise the signal is “Fair”, which means there is no seasonal reason to rush or to hold off.
  4. Price trend. Alongside the signal, the page shows whether the category’s price index is above or below its level a year ago and how that compares with overall inflation.

The Consumer Price Index measures average prices actually paid across the country. It does not track individual products or stores, so a signal describes the conditions in a category, not the quality of a specific offer. Use the Deal Score checker for that.

The October 2025 index was not published. Where a year-over-year comparison needs that month, the base is estimated from September and November 2025 and the figure is marked “about”.

How the housing market meter is set

The meter is a score from 0 to 100 built from three public measures: months of supply of new homes (40%), the yearly change in the FHFA house price index (35%), and where the current 30-year mortgage rate sits within its 52-week range (25%). Higher scores mean conditions lean towards buyers. It is a summary of national conditions and says nothing about a particular town or property.

Cost ranges for home projects

Ranges on the quote pages are typical U.S. installed prices compiled from published 2026 cost guides. They are intentionally wide. Your own quotes will depend on your region, your home and the materials you choose.

Editorial rules

  • We do not publish invented deals, prices, reviews or testimonials.
  • Sponsored placements are labelled as sponsored.
  • Advertisers and partners do not see or influence signals, calendars or guides before publication.
  • External links go to the retailer’s or agency’s own page. Where a link earns us a commission, the page says so.
  • Corrections are made as soon as an error is confirmed. Report one through the contact page.

What this site is not

Home.Deals provides general information. It is not financial, legal, tax or construction advice, and it is not a lender, broker, insurer or contractor.
