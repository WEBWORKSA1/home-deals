# Home.Deals — the concept and why it was chosen

## The decision

**Home.Deals is a timing engine for home spending, with a quote desk attached.** It tells people when to buy for the home (live buy-or-wait signals, a sale calendar, price indexes), helps them check an offer (calculators), and routes the expensive decisions, such as a roof, solar, HVAC or a remodel, into a quote request.

It is deliberately not a deal feed.

## Options considered

| Option | What it needs | Why it lost or won |
|--------|---------------|--------------------|
| Deal feed (a Slickdeals or DealNews clone) | A posting community or affiliate product feeds, plus daily editorial labour | Sixteen incumbents were read in research. A new feed starts empty. Filling it by copying other sites' feeds is scraped content, which risks AdSense rejection and copyright complaints. |
| Coupon code site | Code verification every day | Dominated by a few large operators. Low trust, low margin. |
| Property "home deals" (foreclosures, listings) | Licensed listing data | No free, lawful data source. Regulated. |
| **Timing engine plus quote desk** | Public data and original editorial | **Chosen.** Nothing read in research combines a live seasonal signal with public price data. It can be automated with key-free government data. It is original content, which AdSense requires. It captures the visitors with the highest commercial value. |

## Where the money comes from

Listed by expected value per visitor, highest first. Figures marked as assumptions are planning ranges from general industry knowledge. They were not measured for this site and should be replaced with real numbers once traffic exists.

1. **Quote requests.** A homeowner asking for roofing, solar, HVAC or window quotes is worth far more than an ad impression. Assumption: providers and lead networks pay roughly $20 to $100 or more for an exclusive, qualified request in those trades, less for moving, security and painting. This is the reason the quote wizard is on the home page, in the header of every page and on twelve service pages.
2. **Display advertising (AdSense).** Home improvement and home finance are well-paid ad categories. Assumption: page RPM of roughly $8 to $20, higher on rates and service pages.
3. **Sponsorship.** Category, calculator, alerts email and giveaway sponsorships sold directly through the contact page.
4. **Affiliate links.** Not live at launch. The Deal Radar links are plain links. Affiliate programs can be added per retailer and disclosed.
5. **Reader support.** A support page with a pledge form, ready for payment links.

### A worked example, to show the shape and not to promise a result

At 30,000 visits a month, under the assumptions above:

| Stream | Arithmetic | Monthly range |
|--------|-----------|---------------|
| AdSense | 30,000 visits × 1.6 pages × $8 to $20 RPM | $380 to $960 |
| Quote requests | 30,000 × 0.5% to 1.5% request rate × 30% to 50% placeable × $20 to $80 | $900 to $18,000 |

The spread on leads is wide because it depends on having buyers. That is the single most important commercial task after launch: sign quote partners, or connect a lead network, for the four highest-value trades.

## Why the data choices matter

Every automated source is public and key-free, and nearly all of it is U.S. government work:

| Feature | Source | Live reading at launch |
|---------|--------|------------------------|
| Price Pulse | BLS Consumer Price Index item series | Furniture and bedding about −1.3% over the year, major appliances about −1.2%, all items about +4.0% |
| Mortgage rates | Freddie Mac weekly survey via FRED | 30-year 7.11%, 15-year 6.45% (week of 12 November 2026) |
| Housing meter | Census and HUD new-home supply, FHFA price index, rate position | 50 out of 100, balanced |
| Energy defaults | BLS average prices | Electricity $0.19 per kWh |
| Recall Radar | CPSC recall database | 120 home-product recalls in 300 days, 24 in the last 31 days |
| Consumer alerts | FTC feeds | Pricing and rental-fee enforcement actions |
| Videos | Public YouTube channel feeds | About 60 recent videos from 20 channels |

This avoids three problems at once: no licence fees, no scraping of competitors, and no invented deals or prices anywhere on the site.

## What research changed

- Deal sites that people trust state their evidence. So every signal shows the season logic and the price index behind it, and there is a public method page.
- Quote sites hide who will call. So the wizard shows a ballpark first, makes the phone number optional and states in plain words that Home.Deals is an introduction service and may be paid by providers.
- Official energy pages still describe tax credits that ended in 2025. So the guides state the 2026 position and say where rebates still exist.
- Furniture and mattress sellers are permanently "on sale". So the Deal Score checker measures against the usual selling price, not the crossed-out one.

## Risks, stated plainly

1. **Lead revenue needs buyers.** Until partners exist, requests arrive in the inbox and must be placed by hand.
2. **Signals are seasonal first.** The price index is context. It does not grade individual offers, and the method page says so.
3. **One inbox.** Forms deliver by email through a form service. At volume this should move to a CRM or lead-distribution tool.
4. **AdSense approval is Google's decision.** The site meets the published requirements, and it must be reachable on the home.deals domain before applying.
5. **Name.** The domain is descriptive. Search results showed the U.S. "HOME DEALS" and "HOMEDEALS" marks as cancelled or dead, which is not legal clearance. The site carries an independence and trademark notice on every page and uses its own identity.
