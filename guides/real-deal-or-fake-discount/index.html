How to tell a real deal from a fake discount

Five checks that separate a genuine price drop from an inflated “was” price, with a calculator that does the arithmetic.

Smart shopping, 5 minute read

In short

  • A discount only means something against the price people actually paid.
  • Unit price, delivery and required add-ons decide the real cost.
  • If a product is always on sale, the sale price is the price.

A red tag tells you what a retailer wants you to compare. It does not tell you whether the price is good. The percentage on a sale sign is calculated from a reference price the seller chose, and that reference price is the weakest number on the page.

Start with the price that was actually charged

U.S. advertising rules say a former price used in a comparison should be one at which the product was genuinely offered for a reasonable period. In practice many home products spend most of the year “on sale”. A sofa listed at $1,999 and sold at $1,199 every week is a $1,199 sofa.

So check the history. Look at what the same item sold for over the past two or three months, at the same store and at two competitors. A real deal sits clearly below that band. Deal communities and price-tracking tools make this quick, and so does a screenshot habit when you are planning a large purchase.

Compare the unit, not the box

Pack sizes, piece counts and bundle contents change between stores. A 12-piece cookware set may contain five pans and seven lids and utensils. A “value pack” may cost more per unit than the regular size. Divide the price by the thing you care about: per pan, per sheet, per square foot, per load.

Add what the checkout adds

  • Delivery and haul-away. Freight on furniture and appliances can erase a 15% discount.
  • Required parts. Hoses, cords, mounting kits and installation.
  • Memberships. A member price is only a saving if you would join anyway.
  • Financing. Deferred-interest plans charge interest back to day one if the balance is not cleared in time.

Check the model number

Some products are made in a version for one retailer or one sale event. The model number differs by a letter and so does the specification: a smaller battery, a brushed motor, a thinner mattress layer. If you cannot find the exact model number anywhere else, compare by specification instead.

Ask what waiting would cost

Most home categories have a predictable low season. If the next one is a few weeks away and the item is not urgent, the best deal may be the one you have not seen yet. The sale calendar shows the pattern month by month, and Price Pulse shows whether a category is getting cheaper or dearer compared with last year.

Run the numbers

The Deal Score checker takes the sale price, the usual selling price, delivery, tax and any coupon or cashback, and returns the real discount and a plain verdict. It takes under a minute, which is less time than most returns.
