Why home prices follow a calendar, and how to use it

Model years, floor space and weather set the price of furniture, appliances and outdoor goods. Here is the logic behind the best months to buy.

Smart shopping, 5 minute read

In short

  • Retailers discount when they need space, not when you need the product.
  • Buy against the season: cooling in fall, heating in spring, patio at summer’s end.
  • Holiday weekends are strongest for mattresses and major appliances.

Sale calendars look like folklore until you see what drives them. Three forces set the timing of almost every home discount, and once you know them you can predict the low point for a product nobody has written a calendar for.

Force one: the model year

Manufacturers refresh product lines on a schedule. Most major appliances are renewed in September and October, refrigerators in May, furniture collections in February and August, and vacuums in early summer. The weeks before a refresh are when retailers cut prices on the outgoing version, which is usually the same product with last year’s handle.

Force two: floor space

A store can only display so much. Patio sets must leave before holiday stock arrives, and holiday decor must leave before January storage and fitness displays. Bulky seasonal goods are discounted on the retailer’s deadline, in steps, until they are gone. That is why patio furniture is cheapest in September and October and artificial trees on December 26.

Force three: the weather

Demand for air conditioners, heaters, snow blowers and mowers follows the forecast. Prices peak in the first hot or cold week, when stock is short and buyers are urgent. The same products are cheapest when nobody needs them.

Where holidays fit

Presidents’ Day, Memorial Day, the Fourth of July, Labor Day and Black Friday are marketing events laid on top of those cycles. They matter most in categories with wide margins and negotiable extras: mattresses, major appliances and furniture. For small goods, Black Friday week and the big online sale events set the lows.

How to use it

  1. Decide what you need in the next twelve months.
  2. Look up each item’s low season on the calendar.
  3. Note the price now, so you can judge the sale when it comes.
  4. Buy at the low point, and use the waiting time to collect quotes for anything that needs installing.

Timing is not everything. A broken refrigerator cannot wait for May. For urgent purchases, switch from timing to tactics: open-box stock, price matching and bundle offers narrow the gap quickly.
