Home energy savings after the federal tax credits ended

The 25C and 25D credits stopped at the end of 2025. Here is what still pays: utility and state rebates, and the upgrades with the fastest payback.

Home projects, 6 minute read

In short

  • Federal credits for home efficiency and residential clean energy ended for work done after December 31, 2025.
  • Utility rebates and state-run programs still exist and vary by ZIP code.
  • Air sealing, insulation and controls usually pay back before equipment does.

For three years, federal tax credits covered 30% of many home energy upgrades. That changed with legislation signed in July 2025. According to the IRS, the Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit is not allowed for property placed in service after December 31, 2025, and the Residential Clean Energy Credit is not allowed for expenditures made after that date.

What that means in practice

  • Upgrades completed in 2025 or earlier could be claimed on that year’s return using Form 5695.
  • Unused residential clean energy credit from earlier years can still be carried forward.
  • For work done now, do not build a federal credit into the payback. Some government web pages still describe the old rules, so check the date on anything you read.

What still exists

  • Utility rebates. Many electric and gas utilities pay rebates on heat pumps, heat pump water heaters, smart thermostats, insulation and efficient appliances. Some apply the rebate at checkout through a utility marketplace.
  • State programs. Home energy rebate programs are run state by state, with eligibility often tied to income. Availability differs widely, so check your state energy office.
  • Weatherization assistance. Income-qualified households can receive free efficiency work through state and local agencies.

The quickest way to see what applies to you is a ZIP code search in the ENERGY STAR rebate finder, followed by your utility’s own rebate page.

What pays back fastest

With electricity averaging about 0.19 per kilowatt-hour nationally (October 2026, Bureau of Labor Statistics), the order of operations matters more than ever.

  1. Air sealing and attic insulation. Low cost, and it reduces the size of any heating or cooling system you buy later.
  2. Smart or programmable thermostat. Often rebated to near zero.
  3. LED lighting and controls. Small, immediate, permanent.
  4. Heat pump water heater. Uses a fraction of the electricity of a standard electric tank.
  5. Heating and cooling replacement. Best timed for when the old system is near the end of its life.
  6. Solar. Now depends on your electricity rate and your utility’s export rules. Compare several quotes by price per watt.
