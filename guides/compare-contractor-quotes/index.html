How to compare contractor quotes line by line

A method for getting three comparable quotes, reading what is missing from each, and choosing on value instead of the bottom line.

Home projects, 6 minute read

In short

  • Write one scope and give it to every contractor.
  • The cheapest quote is often the one that left something out.
  • Tie payments to finished stages, and keep the deposit small.

Three quotes for the same job can differ by 50%. Usually nobody is cheating. Each contractor priced a different job, because each was given a different description of it. The fix is to control the description.

Write the scope first

One page is enough. State what is being done, where, with which materials or grade of material, what is being removed, and who handles permits and disposal. Send the same page to every contractor and ask them to price exactly that, with alternatives listed separately.

Line the quotes up

Put the quotes side by side and compare these lines:

  • Materials. Brand, product line, grade and quantity
  • Labour. What is included, and what counts as an extra
  • Preparation and demolition. Tear-off, removal and disposal
  • Allowances. The budget assumed for items you have not chosen yet
  • Permits and inspections. Who obtains and pays for them
  • Timeline. Start date, duration and what happens if it slips
  • Warranty. On materials and on workmanship, in writing

When one quote is far lower, find the line that explains it. A missing tear-off, a low tile allowance or no permit is a different job, not a better price.

Check the business

  1. Verify the licence with your state or local licensing board.
  2. Ask for a certificate of liability insurance and, where required, workers’ compensation.
  3. Call two recent customers with a similar project.
  4. Search the business name with your state attorney general or consumer protection office.

Agree how money moves

A fair schedule ties payments to completed stages. Keep the deposit small. Some states cap it: California, for example, limits home improvement deposits to 10% of the contract price or $1,000, whichever is less. Hold back a final payment until the punch list is done, and ask for lien waivers from the contractor and major suppliers as you pay.

Put changes in writing

Changes are where budgets go. Agree before work starts that every change is priced and signed before it is carried out.

Get a ballpark first

Knowing the typical range makes outliers obvious. The quote request page shows an instant ballpark for twelve project types before you talk to anyone.
