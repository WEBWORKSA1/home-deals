Stacking savings: the order that gets the lowest price

Sale price, coupon, rebate, cashback, card offer and price adjustment: how they combine, and the order to apply them.

Smart shopping, 5 minute read

In short

  • Discounts multiply in sequence, so 20% then 10% is 28%, not 30%.
  • Portals and card offers pay on the amount you are charged.
  • A price adjustment after purchase is the most overlooked saving.

The lowest price on a home purchase rarely comes from one discount. It comes from five or six small ones applied in the right order. Each layer is modest, and together they regularly add 10% to 20% on top of the sale price.

The stack, in order

  1. Sale price. Start from a price that is good against its own history. Stacking cannot rescue a bad base price.
  2. Store coupon or promo code. Email sign-up codes, loyalty offers and mover’s coupons from home improvement stores. Most stores accept one code per order.
  3. Manufacturer or utility rebate. Common on appliances, paint, water heaters and thermostats. Submit the same day and keep copies.
  4. Cashback portal. Click through before adding items to the basket. The portal is paid a commission by the retailer and shares part of it with you.
  5. Card-linked offer or category bonus. Activate in your card app first.
  6. Discounted gift cards. Buying a retailer’s gift card below face value, then paying with it.
  7. Price adjustment. If the price falls after you buy, ask for the difference within the retailer’s window.

Why the arithmetic disappoints

Percentages apply one after another. Twenty percent off followed by a 10% coupon is 28% off, because the coupon applies to the reduced price. Cashback is paid on the amount charged before tax, so it shrinks as the other layers grow. The Deal Score checker applies each layer in the right sequence.

What does not stack

  • Two promo codes on one order, at most stores
  • A portal payout with a coupon code that did not come from the portal, in some cases
  • Price matching with a coupon, which many retailers treat as either-or

Keep it proportionate

An hour of stacking is worth it on a $2,000 appliance order and not on a $20 lamp. Use the full stack for the big purchases and a single code for the rest.
